Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We respectfully submit our manuscript entitled \textbf{``MEISCF: Multi-scale
Edge Information Selection with Cross-scale Fusion for Small Object Detection
in UAV Imagery''} for consideration for publication in \textit{IEEE
Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing}.

Small object detection in UAV aerial imagery poses three fundamental
challenges: objects manifest primarily as edge-dominated patterns at
$16{\times}16$ pixel scales, scale variation spans two orders of magnitude
within single frames, and standard multi-scale fusion mechanisms fail to
adaptively weight scale-specific information. Existing methods, including the
current state-of-the-art CF-YOLO (44.9\% mAP@50), address these challenges
only partially.

This paper proposes MEISCF, which integrates three complementary modules into
the YOLOv11 architecture: (1)~\textbf{Multi-scale Edge Information Selection
(MEIS)}, which explicitly captures boundary features at multiple receptive
fields ($16{\times}16$, $32{\times}32$, $64{\times}64$) with adaptive gating;
(2)~\textbf{Cross-scale Sandwich Fusion}, which performs learnable three-level
simultaneous feature integration rather than sequential two-level fusion; and
(3)~\textbf{Feature Recalibration Module (FRM)}, which applies dual-path
channel and spatial attention after fusion to refine already-integrated
multi-scale representations. Combined with a four-phase progressive training
strategy (640\,$\to$\,800\,$\to$\,1024\,$\to$\,1280\,px), MEISCF achieves
\textbf{55.1\% mAP@50} on VisDrone---a +17.6\% improvement over the YOLOv11
baseline and +10.2\% over CF-YOLO---at 105\,FPS on consumer-grade hardware.
Cross-dataset evaluation on UAVDT (+1.8\% AP) further validates
generalization beyond the training benchmark.

We believe this work makes a meaningful contribution to the geoscience and
remote sensing community and is well suited to the scope of \textit{IEEE
TGRS}. This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submitted version.
AI-assisted tools were used for manuscript editing and figure preparation, in
accordance with IEEE authorship guidelines.

We appreciate your time and look forward to the reviewers' comments.
